FARU vs RideAlong

Same idea. Different math.

Both aim at ride home with students, not strangers. RideAlong charges you every trip — base fare, per-mile rate, a 7.25% platform fee, plus card processing. FARU is a campus message board: core rides and safety stay free, and we never take a cut of your gas share.

Bottom line

If you take more than one long ride home a semester, RideAlong’s meter adds up fast. FARU+ at $4.99/mo (or $49.99/yr) is still less than one Uber ride home — and the free tier covers browse, chat, Ride Requests, and SOS without paying a platform fee on every seat.

Where FARU pulls ahead

  • No pay-per-mile tax. RideAlong intracity is about $2.50 base + $0.95–$1.25/mi, then +7.25% fee and card fees. FARU lets classmates agree on gas in chat. We don’t meter your seat.
  • Free core that actually works. Browse campus rides, post (up to 5 open), request seats, Ride Requests, chat, SOS, guardians, check-ins — free for verified students. You’re not forced into a paid ride to try the board.
  • Drivers keep the gas money. FARU takes $0 of seat prices. No Stripe payouts, no gig-driver paperwork. Fill empty seats; split gas; done.
  • Schedule + hometown, not just a trip fare. Class Rides for the school day, corridor alerts for hometown and break, Quick Rides same-day — built for campus life, not only a price quote per mile.
  • Safety tools on free. SOS, guardians / emergency contacts, check-ins, report, and block ship without FARU+. You’re not buying a membership just to feel less alone in a car.
  • Designated Driver networks. Opt in for free; start club / group DD networks on FARU+. That’s campus nightlife covered — not in a per-mile taxi model.

When RideAlong might still make sense

If you want the app to invoice every seat and you only take rare, short campus hops, a per-ride meter can feel simple. For break, Thanksgiving, and weekend hometown trips — the rides that used to mean Uber for 200 miles — FARU’s board + optional membership is built to win on cost and control.
