Guide

Campus carpool for ride home

Campus carpool is simple: students at the same school post seats or Ride Requests for trips they’re already taking — especially hometown weekends, Thanksgiving, and break. You chat first, meet on campus, and share the road (and gas) instead of booking a stranger Uber for 200 miles.

Why students look for it

  • Uber and Lyft get expensive on longer hometown trips.
  • Facebook and Discord rideshare groups are messy and hard to trust.
  • Parents want a safer story than “random driver from a generic app.”

How FARU fits

FARU (Find A Ride University) is a campus message board for verified classmates — not a taxi company. Drivers fill empty seats; riders find a ride home. Core browse, chat, and safety tools stay free; FARU+ adds corridor alerts and Class Rides.

Quick answers

What is campus carpool?

Students at one university share seats on trips they already drive — usually ride home for break or weekends — then chat and meet on campus before they leave.

Is it cheaper than Uber?

For long hometown trips, yes in almost every case. You split gas with classmates instead of paying a metered rideshare fare for hundreds of miles. FARU itself does not take a cut of the seat.

Is it safer than a Facebook group?

FARU is built for verified students at your school, with in-app chat plus SOS, emergency contacts, check-ins, report, and block on the free tier.
